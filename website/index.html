How tool generation works

From a sentence to a printable,
simulation-ready tool.

No CAD skills. You describe intent; the model reasons about geometry, writes parametric code, and gives you something you can both 3D-print and drop into a physics sim.

  1. 01

    Describe intent

    Say what the tool must do and pick a few parameters — material, mounting, size, target robot arm.

  2. 02

    LLM writes the CAD

    An LLM emits a parametric CadQuery script — real B-rep geometry, not a blob of triangles.

  3. 03

    Build the twin

    The script runs in the cloud and streams back a live 3D twin with exposed, named parameters.

  4. 04

    Tune in real time

    Drag any parameter and watch the geometry rebuild. Lock it in when the fit is right.

  5. 05

    Export the files

    Download STL for printing, GLB for the web, and the CadQuery source to keep iterating.

  6. 06

    Train the robot

    Push the tool into the Training Studio and teach a robot to use it — entirely in simulation.

    FAQ

    The honest answers.

    Do I need to know CAD?

    No. You describe what the tool should do and set a few parameters. The model writes the parametric CAD for you; you only ever touch sliders.

    Why CadQuery instead of just generating a mesh?

    CadQuery produces real solid (B-rep) geometry driven by named parameters. That means the tool is editable, manufacturable, and rebuilds cleanly when you change a dimension — a generated mesh can't do any of that.

    What is "sim2real" and why does it matter?

    It's training a policy entirely in simulation and deploying it on a real robot with no fine-tuning. With enough domain randomization, the policy generalizes to the messiness of the real world — so you don't need thousands of expensive real-world demonstrations to get started.

    Which robots are supported?

    The Training Studio targets AgiBot G1 and G2 to start — the embodiments Omnipresent Robotics deploys. A generic ISO-flange option lets you fit tools to other arms.

    Do I have to train a robot to use the tools?

    Not at all. The Foundry stands on its own — generate a tool and download the files. Training is there when you want your robot to actually use what you made.

    Where does the heavy compute run?

    Scene generation, episode rollouts, and policy training run on GPU infrastructure in the cloud (Isaac Sim under the hood). This page is the human layer — you configure and monitor; the cluster does the work.
